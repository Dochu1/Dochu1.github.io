My research combines geometric mechanics with stochastic analysis to build fluid models that keep their physical structure when noise is added, and numerical methods that keep that structure when the models are discretised. This page collects introductions to the modelling and interactive demonstrations of the numerics.

Modelling

Stochastic modelling of fluids

Why we add noise to fluid models, and how to do it without destroying their geometry. It covers stochastic advection by Lie transport (SALT) and stochastic forcing by Lie transport (SFLT), the circulation and energy each one keeps, and how this leads to stochastic wave effects on ocean currents.

Demos

Interactive demonstrations of structure-preserving numerical methods for stochastic and rough dynamical systems. They run live in your browser. Each one integrates the same noise or driving path with a structure-preserving scheme and a standard alternative, so you can watch which conservation laws survive discretisation.

Stochastic multisymplectic PDEs

The nonlinear Schrödinger equation with stochastic transport or stochastic dispersion, written as a stochastic multisymplectic PDE. The Preissman box scheme, midpoint in both time and space, keeps the discrete 2-form conservation law, mass and, for linear problems, momentum to machine precision, while a linearly implicit scheme does not.

Launch demo

Rough-path-driven adjoint systems

Gradient-based data assimilation for a pendulum driven by a fractional Brownian path. The rough symplectic partitioned Runge–Kutta pairing keeps the adjoint quasi-conservation law to machine precision and returns the exact gradient of the discrete problem; a mismatched explicit pairing gives biased gradients.
