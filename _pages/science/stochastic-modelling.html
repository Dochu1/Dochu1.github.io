How does one introduce stochasticity into the equations of fluid motion without destroying the geometric structure which they encode? We address this question following Stochastic effects of waves on currents in the ocean mixed layer (D. D. Holm and R. Hu, J. Math. Phys. 62, 073102, 2021; arXiv:2011.12094), in which noise is introduced through constrained variational principles.

Why stochastic models?

When modelling ocean and atmosphere dynamics, one inevitably encounters motions below the resolved scales which nonetheless feed back on the resolved flow. In the ocean mixed layer, for example, surface gravity waves drive Langmuir circulations, which are horizontally oriented pairs of counter-rotating vortex tubes aligned approximately with the wind. These circulations entrain sediment and detritus into the mixed layer, and they affect the dispersion of oil spills. Most studies of the ocean mixed layer are performed in the context of wave-averaged dynamics, under the assumption that the surface waves are the fastest component of the system and are only weakly modulated by the currents. Averaging the stratified Euler equations over the wave period yields the Craik–Leibovich (CL) equations, in which the waves act on the currents through the Stokes drift and an additional vortex force.

Averaging, however, discards the variability of the waves. The inclusion of stochasticity is natural in modelling the effects of such unresolvable fluctuations. Namely, each solution of a stochastic model represents one realisation of the flow, and an ensemble of solutions provides a framework for uncertainty quantification and data assimilation. As Lorenz put it, "climate is what you expect, weather is what you get." The question underlying the present approach is then the following.

What types of noise can be added to fluid dynamics while preserving energy conservation, Kelvin–Noether circulation dynamics, and the conservation laws that come from invariance under relabelling of Lagrangian fluid parcels?

Geometry first: Hamilton's principle and circulation

The non-dissipative dynamics of ideal fluids follows from Hamilton's principle \(\delta S = 0\) for an action \(S = \int \ell(u,a)\,dt\), where the Lagrangian \(\ell\) depends on the Eulerian velocity \(u\) and on a set of advected quantities \(a\), such as buoyancy and mass density. The Lagrangian is invariant under relabelling of the Lagrangian fluid parcels, and reduction by this symmetry yields the Euler–Poincaré equations,

$$ \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\delta \ell}{\delta u} + \mathrm{ad}^*_u \frac{\delta \ell}{\delta u} - \frac{\delta \ell}{\delta a}\diamond a = 0, \qquad \frac{d}{dt}a + \mathcal{L}_u a = 0, $$

where \(\mathcal{L}_u\) denotes the Lie derivative with respect to \(u\), and the diamond (\(\diamond\)) operator is defined by duality with the Lie derivative. Noether's theorem for the relabelling symmetry gives the Kelvin–Noether circulation theorem. For the incompressible Euler equations, this is Kelvin's theorem: the circulation \(\oint_{c(u)} \mathbf{u}\cdot d\mathbf{x}\) around any closed loop moving with the flow is conserved. Upon Legendre transformation, the same equations take Lie–Poisson form for the momentum \(m = \delta\ell/\delta u\) with Hamiltonian \(h(m)\). Thus, energy conservation and circulation dynamics arise from a single geometric structure, and stochastic perturbations which respect this structure should be introduced at the level of the variational principle.

Two geometric ways to add noise

Noise in the transport: SALT

Stochastic advection by Lie transport (SALT), introduced by Holm (2015), makes the Lagrangian trajectories of fluid parcels stochastic. The defining feature of SALT is that the transport velocity is the sum of the resolved drift velocity \(u\) and a number of stochastic corrections, given by spatial modes \(\xi_k(\mathbf{x})\) which model the effects of the unresolved scales. Namely,

$$ \mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_t = \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_t,t)\,dt + \sum_k \boldsymbol{\xi}_k(\mathbf{x}_t)\circ dW^k_t . $$

where \(\circ\) denotes Fisk–Stratonovich integration. Momentum, buoyancy and all other advected quantities are transported along these stochastic paths. The Stratonovich form preserves the standard chain and product rules, so that the variational derivation proceeds as in the deterministic case. Consequently, Kelvin's theorem holds on material loops moving with the stochastic flow, and all the Casimirs, such as helicity in 3D and enstrophy in 2D, are preserved. The energy, however, is not conserved in general.

Noise as a force: SFLT

Complementary to SALT, Holm and Hu (2021) introduced stochastic forcing by Lie transport (SFLT). The starting point is a stochastic version of the reduced Lagrange–d'Alembert–Pontryagin principle, in which noise enters as a prescribed stochastic force \(F_i\). This yields the stochastically forced Lie–Poisson equation,

$$ \mathrm{d}m + \mathrm{ad}^*_u m\,dt + \sum_i F_i \circ dW^i_t = 0, \qquad u = \frac{\delta h}{\delta m} . $$

The choice of \(F_i\) determines which conservation laws are retained. Choosing the force to be the Lie transport of prescribed one-form densities \(f_i\), that is, \(F_i = \mathrm{ad}^*_u f_i\), yields an energy-preserving model, since

$$ \mathrm{d}h = \Big\langle \mathrm{d}m, \frac{\delta h}{\delta m}\Big\rangle = \Big\langle -\mathrm{ad}^*_u m\,dt - \sum_i \mathrm{ad}^*_u f_i\circ dW^i_t,\; u\Big\rangle = 0, $$

by the antisymmetry of the commutator, \(\mathrm{ad}_u u = -[u,u] = 0\). In vector calculus notation, for incompressible flow, the stochastic term reads

$$ \sum_i \Big( \mathbf{u}\times \mathrm{curl}\,\mathbf{f}_i - \nabla(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{f}_i) \Big)\circ dW^i_t , $$

which is a stochastic vortex force of the Craik–Leibovich type, together with a stochastic contribution to the pressure gradient. The variational origin of the model ensures that a Kelvin–Noether circulation theorem still holds. Alternatively, the choice \(F_i = \mathrm{ad}^*_{\delta C/\delta m} f_i\) preserves a chosen Casimir \(C\), such as helicity, in place of the energy.

Waves and currents in the ocean mixed layer

Applying energy-preserving SFLT to the Euler–Boussinesq equations yields a stochastic extension of the CL theory. Taking the first noise mode to be the steady Stokes drift, \(\mathbf{f}^0 = -\mathbf{u}^S(\mathbf{x})\) with \(dW^0_t = dt\), the equations of motion become

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\mathbf{u} &= \mathbf{u}\times\mathrm{curl}\big(\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}^S(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{x})\big)\,dt - \nabla\Big(p + \tfrac12|\mathbf{u}|^2 + \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{u}^S\Big)dt \\ &\quad - gb\,\hat{\mathbf{z}}\,dt + \sum_{k>0}\Big(\mathbf{u}\times\mathrm{curl}\,\mathbf{f}^k - \nabla(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{f}^k)\Big)\circ dW^k_t , \end{aligned} $$ $$ \partial_t D + \mathrm{div}(D\mathbf{u}) = 0 \ \text{ with } D = 1, \qquad \partial_t b + \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla b = 0 , $$

where \(b\) is the buoyancy, \(p\) is the pressure and \(2\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \mathrm{curl}\,\mathbf{R}\) is the Coriolis parameter. These equations admit a clear physical interpretation. Namely, the Stokes drift is augmented into the semimartingale

$$ \mathrm{d}\mathbf{u}^S = \mathbf{u}^S(\mathbf{x})\,dt + \sum_k \mathbf{f}^k(\mathbf{x})\circ dW^k_t , $$

so that the noise in the waves feeds into the currents through a stochastic vortex force, whilst the total energy is preserved. The spatial modes \(\mathbf{f}^k\) are to be calibrated from data. When the stochastic terms are absent, one recovers the deterministic CL equations.

The full model also admits stochastic sources \(a^k\) in the buoyancy equation, which model stochastic changes in the material properties of fluid parcels, such as the entrainment and detrainment of sediment, algae blooms, oil droplets or microplastics by Langmuir circulations. The circulation then satisfies the stochastic Kelvin theorem,

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\oint_{c(u)}(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{R})\cdot d\mathbf{x} &= \oint_{c(u)} gz\,\nabla b\cdot d\mathbf{x}\,dt \\ &\quad - \sum_k\oint_{c(u)}\Big((\mathrm{curl}\,\mathbf{f}^k)\times\mathbf{u} - gz\,\nabla a^k\Big)\cdot d\mathbf{x}\circ dW^k_t . \end{aligned} $$

Hence, the stochastic vortex force and the buoyancy sources generate circulation locally, which provides a mechanism for stochastic Langmuir circulations. The potential vorticity (PV) \(q = (\boldsymbol{\omega} + 2\boldsymbol{\Omega})\cdot\nabla b\) satisfies

$$ \mathrm{d}q + \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla q\,dt = -\sum_k \mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{J}^k\circ dW^k_t , $$

which is a conservation law in flux form. That is, the stochastic "J-fluxes" \(\mathbf{J}^k\) redistribute PV locally, whilst the total PV \(Q = \int_{\mathcal D} q\,dV\) is preserved for appropriate boundary conditions. Numerical simulations of these equations, and the calibration of \(\mathbf{f}^k\) from data, were left for future work in the original paper.

Expectations and fluctuations

Holm and Hu (2021) also introduced an Eulerian averaged version of SFLT (EA SFLT), in which the Eulerian solutions are decomposed into their expectations and fluctuations. For quadratic Hamiltonians, the expectation satisfies a closed deterministic equation, along which the energy of the expected flow decreases. Indeed, this energy is transferred to the fluctuations, whilst the total energy is conserved. One thus obtains a conservative, geometric description of the exchange of energy between the mean flow and its variability, in the spirit of Lorenz's remark.

From models to computation

Stochastic models derived in this way inherit their conservation laws and variational structure from their deterministic counterparts. A numerical method which does not respect this structure introduces spurious drifts in energy, circulation or other invariants, which cannot be distinguished from the physical effects of the noise. Our subsequent work therefore develops structure-preserving discretisations for these models, namely stochastic multisymplectic integrators for stochastic partial differential equations (PDEs), and symplectic methods for rough adjoint systems arising in sensitivity analysis and data assimilation. Both may be explored in the demos.
