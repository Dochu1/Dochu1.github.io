The nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation with stochastic transport or stochastic dispersion is a stochastic multisymplectic PDE: it comes from a stochastic variational principle, and its solutions satisfy a stochastic 2-form conservation law in space and time. Below, the same noise drives two discretisations. The top panel uses the multisymplectic box scheme (midpoint in time and in space), the bottom one a linearly implicit scheme.

Each panel shows the cell averages \(|\psi|\) on \(x\in[-16,16)\) (135 cells) with time running upwards; the newest time is at the top.

  • Multisymplectic box scheme (midpoint in time and space)
  • Linearly implicit (cubic term explicit)

The model

Write \(\psi = p + iq\). We solve, on a periodic domain,

$$ \mathrm{d}\psi = i\big(\psi_{xx} + 2\gamma|\psi|^2\psi\big)\,\mathrm{d}t \;+\; i\,\varepsilon\,\psi_{xx}\circ \mathrm{d}W^D_t \;-\; \sigma\Big(\xi\,\psi_x + \tfrac12\xi_x\,\psi\Big)\circ \mathrm{d}W^T_t . $$
  • Stochastic transport (\(\sigma>0\)): \(\psi\) is carried by the vector field \(\xi(x)\) as a half-density. The noise Hamiltonian is \(\int \xi\,\mathrm{Im}(\bar\psi\psi_x)\,\mathrm{d}x\), the momentum density paired with \(\xi\).
  • Stochastic dispersion (\(\varepsilon>0\)): the dispersion coefficient fluctuates in time, with noise Hamiltonian \(\int |\psi_x|^2\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Both noises enter through the same variational and Hamiltonian structure as the drift. As a result the equation is a stochastic multisymplectic PDE \(K\,\mathrm{d}z + L z_x\,\mathrm{d}t = \nabla S(z)\,\mathrm{d}t + \nabla S_1(z)\circ \mathrm{d}W\) with \(z = (p, q, p_x, q_x)\). It satisfies the stochastic 2-form conservation law \(\mathrm{d}\omega + \partial_x\kappa\,\mathrm{d}t = 0\), where \(\omega = \tfrac12\,\mathrm{d}z\wedge K\mathrm{d}z\) and \(\kappa = \tfrac12\,\mathrm{d}z\wedge L\mathrm{d}z\). It also conserves the mass \(M = \int|\psi|^2\,\mathrm{d}x\) pathwise. By the stochastic Noether theorem, it conserves the momentum \(P = \int \mathrm{Im}(\bar\psi\psi_x)\,\mathrm{d}x\) whenever the noise is translation invariant: constant \(\xi\), or stochastic dispersion.

The box scheme

Write the equation as a first-order system in \(z = (p, q, v, w)\) with \(\psi = p + iq\), \(v = p_x\) and \(w = q_x\). On every space-time cell \([x_j, x_{j+1}]\times[t_n, t_{n+1}]\), the Preissman box scheme replaces \(\partial_t\) by \(\delta_t M_x\) and \(\partial_x\) by \(\delta_x M_t\), with

$$ \delta_x u_j = \frac{u_{j+1}-u_j}{\Delta x}, \qquad M_x u_j = \frac{u_{j+1}+u_j}{2}, $$

and the same in time. Every right-hand side, including the noise terms, is evaluated at the cell centre. This is the midpoint rule in time and in space, the lowest-order member of the collocation family. Eliminating \(v, w\) shows that the cell averages \(\phi = M_x\psi\) evolve by the implicit midpoint rule in time, with the box derivative \(D = \delta_x M_x^{-1}\) in space. \(D\) is skew-adjoint, with symbol \(\tfrac{2i}{\Delta x}\tan(\pi k/N)\). Because the box operators are circulant on the periodic grid, each implicit stage is solved with discrete Fourier transforms. \(N\) is odd so that \(M_x\) is invertible; an even \(N\) leaves a spurious checkerboard mode.

What the plots show

  • 2-form. With periodic boundaries, the local law integrates to conservation of the symplectic form \(\omega(\delta_1,\delta_2) = \int \mathrm{Im}(\bar\delta_1\delta_2)\,\mathrm{d}x\) for any two solutions \(\delta_1,\delta_2\) of the linearised equation. We propagate two such variations alongside \(\psi\), each with the linearisation of its own scheme. The box scheme keeps \(\omega\) at round-off. The linearly implicit scheme does not, even though it treats every linear term with the midpoint rule.
  • Mass is a quadratic invariant, so the midpoint rule in time conserves the discrete mass \(\sum_j|\phi_j|^2\Delta x\) exactly. Taking the cubic term explicitly breaks this.
  • Momentum. For the linear equation (untick the cubic term) with translation-invariant noise, the box scheme also conserves discrete momentum exactly. With the cubic term, the box scheme conserves momentum only approximately, and with \(\xi(x)\) the exact flow does not conserve it at all. For the linear equation the two schemes coincide, because only the treatment of the cubic term differs.

The Brownian increments are truncated at \(\pm\sqrt{2|\ln\Delta t|\,\Delta t}\), which keeps the implicit stages solvable without changing the order of convergence.
